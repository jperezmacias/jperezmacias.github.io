3D Audio - Introduction

3D Sound

4.0. INTRODUCTION

Imagine closing your eyes while standing on a busy street corner. In that instant, you can easily perceive cars passing from left to right, footsteps approaching behind you, someone speaking to your right, and an airplane passing overhead. Human hearing naturally localizes sounds in full three-dimensional space.

The goal of 3D spatial audio technology is to recreate this rich acoustic sensation artificially over headphones or loudspeakers, making listeners perceive sound sources as originating from specific three-dimensional coordinates in space.

Applications of 3D Audio Technology

  • Virtual Reality & Simulation: Flight simulators, spatial teleconferencing, and immersive military communication systems.
  • Entertainment & Gaming: Interactive spatial audio engines that adjust source positions dynamically based on player orientation.
  • Music & Auditory Display: Electroacoustic music composition, binaural recordings, and data sonification.

Audio Playback and Recording Systems

  1. Binaural Recording (Dummy Head): Microphones placed inside the ear canals of an artificial acoustic manikin record sound exactly as human ears receive it.
  2. HRTF Synthesis: Filtering dry mono audio with digital filters derived from measured head transfer functions to position virtual sounds in space.
  3. Multichannel Loudspeaker Arrays: Using Ambisonics, Vector Base Amplitude Panning (VBAP), or Wave Field Synthesis (WFS) across multi-speaker installations.
